You ask Claude for "the same page in dark mode" and it gives you something
that is technically dark — `bg-gray-900`, `text-gray-300`, every icon now
a slightly desaturated version of itself — but the page has lost its
spine. The contrast feels flat. The accent colour is mud. The whole
thing reads as **light mode in a costume**, not as a design that was
built for the dark.

This is one of the most common shapes of AI slop, and it has a
specific cause: the model is treating dark mode as a transform of the
light theme. Same hierarchy, same accent, same shadow recipe — just
inverted. That is not what good dark mode is, and that is why it keeps
coming out wrong.

## What "dark mode is its own design" actually means

A real dark theme is not "light theme but `--bg: #1a1a1a`." It is a
parallel set of decisions about contrast, depth, and accent. The
canonical version of this argument is the Material 3 "elevation by
surface" model — surfaces get **lighter** as they come forward, because
you cannot cast a shadow on a black background.

If your AI-generated dark mode uses `box-shadow` to lift cards off
the background, that is the giveaway. Shadows do not exist in the
dark. **Light does.**

<Callout tone="info" title="The mental model that fixes this">
  In light mode, depth comes from shadow. In dark mode, depth comes
  from lightness. A "raised" card on a dark background is **lighter**
  than the surface beneath it, not darker with a glow.
</Callout>

## The four tells that you have AI dark mode, not dark mode

When you scan the screenshot the model handed back, look for these
four things. Every one of them is a fingerprint:

1. **Pure black background** (`#000` or `#0a0a0a`). Real dark themes
   sit around `#1a1a1a`–`#1f2937` because OLED-pure-black sucks the
   life out of every other colour next to it. If the rest of your UI
   has any blue or warm tint, the background should match.

2. **Light-mode accent dragged into dark unchanged.** The indigo that
   pops on a white background often turns to **navy mud** on a dark one.
   Dark-mode accents almost always need to be **lighter and less
   saturated** than their light-mode counterparts.

3. **Body text at `#fff` or `gray-100`.** Pure white on dark backgrounds
   buzzes. Real product dark modes use something like `gray-200` or
   `rgba(255,255,255,0.87)` so the text sits *on* the page rather than
   floating in front of it.

4. **Borders set with opacity instead of a tinted gray.** `border-white/10`
   is the AI's tell. It works, but it looks the same on every page on
   the internet. A border set as `rgb(38 43 56)` reads as deliberate.

## The prompt patterns that actually fix it

The fix is to **stop asking for "the dark mode version."** Ask for a
dark theme as if you were briefing a new designer who has never seen
the light version:

<CodeBlock
  title="Prompt scaffold for a real dark theme"
  language="text"
  code={`Design a dark theme for this page. Do not start from the light theme.

Background: #14161a (a neutral-warm dark, not pure black)
Surface (cards, panels): #1c1f24 (lighter than bg — that is how
  elevation works in the dark)
Body text: rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.86)
Muted text: rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.56)
Accent: shift the light-mode #4f46e5 indigo up the lightness axis
  to #818cf8. Same hue, more luminance. Brighter, not bluer.
Borders: rgb(38 43 56). Not white-with-opacity.
No box-shadows on raised elements. Use the lighter surface colour
  instead.

The whole theme should look like it was designed for the dark, not
ported from the light. If something feels like an inverted version
of its light counterpart, change it.`}
/>

The last paragraph is the one that does the most work. Explicit
permission to **diverge from the light theme** is what stops the model
from doing a mechanical inversion.

## A reading test for any dark mode

Once the model returns something, do this 15-second test before
shipping:

<Checklist
  storageKey="blog-dark-mode-test"
  title="The 15-second dark mode check"
  items={[
    { id: 'bg', label: 'Is the background NOT pure black? (#1a-ish, not #000)' },
    { id: 'accent', label: 'Does the accent colour look brighter than its light-mode version?' },
    { id: 'depth', label: 'Are raised surfaces LIGHTER than the page, not lifted with a shadow?' },
    { id: 'body', label: 'Is the body text slightly off-white, not pure #fff?' },
    { id: 'border', label: 'Are dividers tinted gray, not white-with-opacity?' }
  ]}
/>

If you cannot answer yes to four of the five, you have AI dark mode,
not dark mode.

## The deeper rule

The same principle that fixed the first article's AI slop applies here:
**dark mode is a decision, not a transform.** The moment you treat it
as "the same thing with the colours flipped," you have already lost the
distinct character that makes a good dark theme worth shipping.

The model will happily do the inversion if you ask for the inversion.
Ask instead for the **design**, and tell it that the dark theme has
its own opinions.
